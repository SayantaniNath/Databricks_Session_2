Why each choice is defensible in the room

ChoiceThe trade-off to articulate
Iceberg on S3, read by SnowflakeOpen table format = no lock-in, multiple engines on one copy, but you give up some Snowflake-native performance features and take on maintenance (compaction, snapshot expiry) yourself. She already knows the Delta/Iceberg/Hudi log differences cold from 2C — this is where that pays off.
ELT not ETLPush transformation into the warehouse: cheaper to iterate, full lineage, testable. Costs you compute on raw data you may never use, and needs governance to stop the raw layer becoming a swamp.
dbt snapshots for SCD2Declarative history vs hand-rolled MERGE. Closes her documented SCD2 weak spot with a hands-on artifact.
Airflow over DagsterMaturity and ecosystem vs Dagster's asset-centric model and better data-aware scheduling. Know the difference; don't claim Dagster hands-on.
Redis serving layerDirect callback to Citi. Sub-ms point lookups the warehouse can't serve; costs you a second consistency story.

3. Getting genuine volume — cheaply

Two independent moves. Do both; they answer different questions.

3.1 Data she owns end-to-end — DuckDB TPC-H

DuckDB can generate TPC-H locally with no download:

INSTALL tpch; LOAD tpch;
CALL dbgen(sf = 10);          -- ~60M lineitem rows, ~10 GB
COPY lineitem TO 's3://.../lineitem/' (FORMAT parquet, PARTITION_BY (l_shipdate));

SF10 = ~60M fact rows, ~10 GB — real volume, fits a laptop, generates in minutes. Go to SF100 (~600M rows, ~100 GB) only if disk allows; SF10 is enough to be credible and to make partitioning/clustering decisions actually matter.

3.2 Terabyte-scale tuning — Snowflake's free sample data

Every Snowflake account ships with SNOWFLAKE_SAMPLE_DATA, including TPCH_SF1000 and TPCDS_SF10TCL (10 TB). Storage is free — you pay only compute. So she can run real query-tuning and warehouse-sizing experiments against terabyte tables without generating or storing anything.

This is the highest-value hour in the whole build. Run the same query on XS / S / M / L warehouses against a TB table. Record runtime, credits, and where the curve stops paying. Then run one query with and without a well-chosen cluster key and record partitions pruned. Those two experiments give her measured numbers to quote for warehouse sizing, micro-partition pruning, and the cost/performance trade-off — the exact questions a Snowflake-heavy architect interview asks, and currently her single biggest gap.
Credit warning. A trial gives $400 / 30 days — ample, but a Large warehouse on a 10 TB table burns it fast. Use XS by default, set AUTO_SUSPEND = 60, always LIMIT exploratory queries, and set a resource monitor before touching SF10TCL.

7. How to talk about it

"At Citi I was one of the leads creating the data model for our Data Acquisition Platform — regulated data, four domains, twenty-plus data flows, and I owned delivery on it. Logical and physical design, the SQL, and a governance layer for data-concern remediation. The stack underneath was Oracle and Talend, at hundreds of thousands to low millions of rows per table. The problems that platform had — late-arriving data, access control on regulated fields, keeping a model coherent across domains with competing owners — are the same problems at Ōura's scale, just with different tools. So I rebuilt the pattern end to end on Snowflake, dbt, Iceberg and Airflow, as a reference implementation rather than production, to find out what actually changes. Two things surprised me: [measured finding], and [where the modern tool was worse or added a cost]."
Note what this version does: it leads with design authority and delivery ownership, which is what an architect req is buying, and it's honest about scale rather than hiding it. Naming "hundreds of thousands to low millions of rows" before they ask converts a weakness into evidence you calibrate accurately — and it sets up the build as the deliberate answer to that gap. Volunteering the limitation is a strength move here, not a concession.
That last clause is what separates this from a tutorial follow-along. Have two concrete findings ready — one where the new stack clearly won, one where it introduced a cost the old one didn't. Candidates who only report the happy path read as having followed instructions. Candidates who name a downside read as having actually made decisions.
Never claim: production scale on this build · Dagster or Pulumi hands-on (not built) · BigQuery/Vertex · anything you didn't measure yourself. Quote only real numbers. The ClinicalFlow lesson stands — the honest figure beats the impressive one, because the impressive one collapses on the first follow-up question.
